I finished writing this account on July 25, 2002.

Heliodoro Rodríguez S., age 81.
Guillermina Roldán, age 76.

Before beginning this account, my wife and I give thanks to God our Lord for allowing us to reach this day. With God's blessing, we celebrate 56 years of happy married life.

Heliodoro Rodríguez Sarabia was born on June 6, 1921, in the village of San Lorenzo Tlalmimilolpan, in the municipality of San Juan Teotihuacán, State of Mexico.

My parents are Aurelio Rodríguez Rivero and Josefina Sarabia Celis.

I was baptized at the Parish of San Juan Bautista on July 3, 1921. My godparents were Mr. Carlos Arce and Mrs. Natalia Mondragón of San Juan Teotihuacán.

I did my first years of study at the village school. It was one room with only one teacher, who taught first, second, and third grades. There were no desks, so we sat on wooden beams. In those days we did not use pencils or notebooks; we wrote on a stone slate with a slate pencil.

To continue my studies, my parents enrolled me in a private school in San Juan Teotihuacán. The school was called Ma. Elena Alonso Arce.

I finished primary school there. I continued at the same school's academy for two more years, through 1936, taking classes in shorthand, singing, and a little theater.

I must have been five or six when there was a gunfight in San Juan between two cavalry regiments of the Mexican Army, a consequence of the Revolution of 1910.

At age nine, I took part in the first population census in our community of San Lorenzo, in 1930. The census counted 436 residents.

My father, Mr. Aurelio Rodríguez R., had been appointed to do the census work, but because of unavoidable circumstances he could not go and sent me as his representative.

In 1944 I traveled to the port of Veracruz in a 1936 Ford. The whole Rodríguez Sarabia family went: my father, my mother, my brother Gonzalo, and my sisters Aurelia and Concha. It took us nine days to return.

The Ranchito was left in the care of a very trusted employee, Guillermo Sánchez. When we returned, everything was in perfect order.

In 1945, when Father José Ma. Aguilar was parish priest in San Juan, he invited me to visit his birthplace, La Mora, Michoacán. From there I went to Guadalajara, Jalisco. The Mexico–Guadalajara highway had just opened, running through Toluca, Zitácuaro, and Mil Cumbres.

In 1940 I met a young woman named Guillermina Roldán, and I fell in love with her.

Our courtship lasted five years. In July 1946, God our Lord allowed us to celebrate our marriage under both civil and religious law, on Thursday, July 25, 1946.

When my parents realized I had a girlfriend, they considered the consequences of the courtship and decided to buy a plot of land to build a home for their son Heliodoro.

Construction of the house began in 1944 and was completed in 1946.

The house is at No. 5 Campos Elíseos, on the corner of No. 5 Paseo de las Flores, in San Lorenzo.

God our Lord gave us 12 children through this marriage. Nine are living and three died when they were very young.

In 1946, 1947, 1948, and 1949, I served as president of the Pro-Literacy Committee in the community of San Lorenzo.

On a trip we made to Guadalajara in 1948.

In 1950, my uncle Manuel and my aunts Juanita and Beatrisita, who lived in Mexico City, asked me to look after two large framed photographs of my aunts, grandmother, and great-grandmother, so I could keep them as a family remembrance. Those pictures are still in my home, and I continue to care for them.

My father, Mr. Aurelio Rodríguez, died in 1952.

In 1950, while Mr. Filemón García Godines, a resident of our community, was municipal president, a committee was appointed to make the necessary arrangements with the Central Light and Power Company to request electricity for San Lorenzo. The committee was made up of:
President: Aurelio Rodríguez Rivero
Secretary: Felipe Castañeda Alonso
Treasurer: Heliodoro Rodríguez Sarabia

The process took three years and we faced many problems. Other people had started the work in earlier years, but they did not have the patience to see the process through and left everything abandoned.

As the committee's treasurer, I, Heliodoro Rodríguez S., worked hard to bring the effort to completion. In June 1952, electricity service was inaugurated in San Lorenzo Tlalmimilolpan.

General Ignacio M. Beteta, accompanied by his family and the municipal authorities of Teotihuacán, unveiled the commemorative plaque. At that time, the municipal president was Lic. Rafael Monterota y Salceda. Community authorities and residents were also there.

Don Pedro Ruiz Gutiérrez of San Juan Teotihuacán gave me valuable help with these arrangements. He introduced me at the office of Lic. Don Ramón Beteta, Secretary of Finance and Public Credit. From there I was sent with someone who took me to the offices where I had to complete all the paperwork to bring electricity to San Lorenzo.

1943

The municipal authorities of San Juan Teotihuacán appointed me to make a census of all the young men who were 18 and fit for national military service.

This responsibility caused me many problems with my neighbors. They thought I was turning the young men over to the government to send them to war, because the Second World War had begun at that time.

On February 5, 1952, I was elected the first municipal delegate of this community of San Lorenzo.

My daughters Yolanda and Estela studied at the school run by the sisters. My son Aurelio is also in this photograph. It was taken in 1956.

In January 1959, I was appointed treasurer of the Community Improvement Board for Civic and Material Betterment in San Lorenzo. The appointment was signed by the State Governor, Dr. Gustavo Baz, and the Secretary General of Government, Dr. Jorge Jiménez Cantú.

I took part as a board member in the community drainage works in 1971 and 1972.

In 1976, the renovation of my house began. The arches were built, and the work was completed in 1983.

On June 25, 1971, my wife and I celebrated 25 years of marriage. To give thanks to God our Lord for all the blessings we had received, we asked for a thanksgiving Mass. It was held at the church of San Lorenzo, with all our children beside us, and we gave thanks to God our Lord for the peace and calm in our family.

Coral Anniversary
35 years of marriage
July 25, 1981

All my children organized the celebration of our Coral Anniversary. Thanks be to God, it was a beautiful event, thanks to the goodwill of all my children.

In 1978 and 1979, I was treasurer for the purchase of an organ and ten benches for the church. The whole community contributed.

1982–1983

I was treasurer of the church stewardship committee. When the stewards changed, I handed the incoming stewards the remaining money from the collection used to pay for the two festivals of San Lorenzo and Santa Catarina.

Mr. Isaías Rivas, representing the new stewards, received $16,000.00 in cash from me, Heliodoro Rodríguez, in the presence of the municipal delegate, Mr. Ricardo Torres. I still keep the receipt for that amount.

1988–1990

During Father Enrique Buendía's time as parish priest, a group of volunteers formed to prepare to serve the sick in the parish as Extraordinary Ministers of Holy Communion.

My wife and I were part of this volunteer group. In 1990, the Bishop of Texcoco, Magín Torreblanca Reyes, came to the parish of San Juan to commission the group that had prepared as Ministers of Holy Communion. We were sent to all the villages in the parish to care for the sick.

My wife and I served in our community of San Lorenzo from 1991 to 2000.

1992

That year I took part in the work to decorate the church sanctuary in gold in our community of San Lorenzo.

During the first years of our marriage, when our first children arrived, I began taking them and my wife to a homeopathic doctor in Mexico City. This led me to learn about homeopathy, and I bought a book that explained something about homeopathic medicine.

With what I had learned, my wife and I were sometimes able to prepare medicines for our children.

At that time we had a shop. Some of our customers noticed that we gave our children medicine for minor complaints and asked us to help their children get well.

There is something more: because there was no one in San Lorenzo who could give injections, I, Heliodoro Rodríguez S., decided to learn how to give intramuscular injections. While I had the shop, I gave injections to some sick people in my community.

My wife and I ran the shop for 18 years. After that, I went to work in Mexico City.

On July 25, 1996, our Golden Anniversary was celebrated. The thanksgiving Mass was held at the chapel of San Juan Teotihuacán. The reception was at our compadre Chano's hall, and my grandchildren organized a stage show.

On July 25, 2001, our 55th anniversary was celebrated. The thanksgiving Mass was held at the church of San Lorenzo, and the reception was at our compadre Chano's hall.

At about five in the morning on January 28, 2002, my wife suffered a stroke. She lost consciousness and could no longer speak; the right side of her body lost movement.

We admitted her to the Social Security Hospital, where she stayed for two days. My son Juan Manuel arranged to transfer her to the Military Hospital. As I write this, she is still receiving treatment. Thanks be to God our Lord, her recovery is progressing, though the doctors said it would be very slow.
